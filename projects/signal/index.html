THE CASE STUDY / SELF-INITIATED CONCEPT

Turn scattered metrics into a decision path.

Below is the design rationale for the working prototype above. This is a proposed solution, not a commissioned project or a validated user-research outcome.

AUDIENCE

Store operators and growth managers who need actionable context without becoming analytics specialists.

DESIGN GOAL

Find a concerning customer segment, understand where its journey changes, and formulate a measurable next experiment.

CONSTRAINTS

Unknown source-data quality, varying purchase cycles, small cohorts, and the risk of presenting correlation as causation.

01 / THE CHALLENGE

What needs to change?

A merchant reviewing retention data must move between summary KPIs and disconnected reports. The design challenge is not simply displaying more data; it is helping someone identify the next question worth investigating.

02 / DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Rules for the experience.

01

Start with a question

Lead with customer segments, not a wall of charts.

02

Keep context visible

Show cohort, time range, and denominator alongside any metric.

03

Separate evidence and action

Distinguish observations from testable hypotheses.

03 / INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

One connected journey.

  1. 01Choose cohort
  2. 02Set time window
  3. 03Inspect journey stage
  4. 04Review evidence
  5. 05Form experiment

Conceptual task flow, represented by the interactive prototype above. No participant testing has been conducted.

04 / DESIGN EXPLORATION

Choices and trade-offs.

DECISION 01

Cohort-first navigation

The initial choice gives the analysis a clear subject; a generic overview would force users to hunt for relevance.

Trade-off: Risk: people may not understand segment definitions; add cohort definitions and tooltips in the next iteration.

DECISION 02

Four-stage journey

A compact sequence creates a readable mental model instead of overwhelming users with an event stream.

Trade-off: Risk: stages may not match every commerce business; the taxonomy should be configurable.

DECISION 03

Hypothesis, not prediction

Actions are framed as experiments, avoiding ungrounded claims of lift.

Trade-off: Risk: additional guidance is needed for statistical interpretation.

05 / DESIGN ITERATION

From dense KPI tables to guided stage diagnostics.

V1 / INITIAL WIREFRAME

Multi-Chart Dashboard

Early wireframes grouped 8 retention charts on one screen, causing cognitive overload and obscuring which segment required immediate attention.

V2 / FINAL SOLUTION

Progressive Stage Selection

Replaced the dense chart wall with a 3-cohort selector and 4-stage horizontal stepper that reveals stage-specific evidence and testable hypotheses on click.

07 / STATES & EDGE CASES

Beyond the happy path.

  • No data / insufficient sample
  • Unusually long purchase cycle
  • Missing customer events
  • Conflicting metric definitions
08 / VALIDATION PLAN

What we would test next.

  • Can a first-time user explain which customers the selected rate represents?
  • Can they identify a drop-off without confusing association with cause?
  • Can they propose one follow-up experiment from the evidence?

Success signals to measure: Task completion, interpretation accuracy, time to first defensible hypothesis.

These are proposed evaluation criteria. No participant interviews, test results, conversion lift, or client impact are represented as completed work.
